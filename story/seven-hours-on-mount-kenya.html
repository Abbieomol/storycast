Seven Hours on Mount Kenya

Video · 1:07

Transcript

Show or hide the transcript

[Visual: Illustrative footage of misty moorland on the lower slopes of Mount Kenya.]

On 20 November 2020, three runners gathered at the Sirimon gate of Mount Kenya, about 2,650 metres above sea level.

[Visual: Illustrative footage of a trail climbing through grass.]

One of them, Kenneth Kemboi, planned to run to Point Lenana and back, carrying everything he needed. The trail climbed through moorland and then into snow, in places almost knee deep.

[Visual: Illustrative footage of snow-covered rocks and a jagged ridge.]

Landmarks he had memorised disappeared under the white, and for about a kilometre he lost the path. He reached the summit in three hours and forty-five minutes.

[Visual: Illustrative footage of a descent in clear light.]

Back at the gate, the clock read about seven hours: forty-nine kilometres, unsupported, with only what he had packed. It became the unsupported fastest known time on that route.

[Visual: Illustrative footage of the summit peaks at sunrise.]

But Kemboi had studied the mountain first, and trained for the effort. Most climbers take several days, and the mountain rewards patience.
